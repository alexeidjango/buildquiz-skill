# The Solar System: lesson notes (demo source)

These short notes are the only source of truth for the demo quiz. Every explanation in the quiz cites a
section as "notes §N".

## §1 What the Solar System is
The Solar System is the Sun and everything bound to it by gravity: eight planets and their moons, dwarf
planets such as Pluto, asteroids and comets.

## §2 The planets in order
From the Sun outwards: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune. The four inner
planets (Mercury to Mars) are small and rocky. The four outer planets are giants: Jupiter and Saturn are
gas giants, Uranus and Neptune are ice giants.

## §3 The largest and the smallest
Jupiter is the largest planet; its mass is more than twice that of all the other planets together.
Mercury is the smallest planet and the closest to the Sun.

## §4 The hottest planet
Venus is the hottest planet, about 465 °C at the surface, because its thick carbon dioxide atmosphere
traps heat. Mercury is closer to the Sun but has almost no atmosphere, so it loses its heat.

## §5 The Sun
The Sun is a star, made mostly of hydrogen and helium. In its core it produces energy by nuclear fusion,
turning hydrogen into helium. Sunlight takes about 8 minutes to reach Earth.

## §6 The Moon
The Moon is Earth's only natural satellite. It does not make its own light: it shines by reflecting
sunlight. It orbits Earth in about 27.3 days, and it turns once on its axis in the same time, so the same
side always faces Earth. The phases repeat about every 29.5 days.

## §7 Days, years and seasons
Earth turns once on its axis in about 24 hours (a day) and orbits the Sun in about 365.25 days (a year).
The seasons are caused by the tilt of Earth's axis, about 23.4°, not by Earth's distance from the Sun.

## §8 Orbits
Planets move around the Sun in ellipses. The farther a planet is from the Sun, the longer its year:
Mercury needs 88 days, Neptune about 165 years.

## §9 Rings
All four giant planets have rings. Saturn's rings are by far the brightest and largest; the rings of
Jupiter, Uranus and Neptune are faint.

## §10 Pluto
In 2006 the International Astronomical Union reclassified Pluto as a dwarf planet, so the Solar System
now has eight planets.
